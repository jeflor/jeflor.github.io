ABOUT

Somewhere between operations and engineering.

I've spent most of my career figuring out how businesses actually work, then making their systems work better.

My background is not a conventional software engineering path. I studied Computer Engineering Technology, but my career took me into CRM systems, marketing operations, integrations, automation, and the messy space between a business process and the software supporting it.

I've worked with platforms such as Keap/Infusionsoft, GoHighLevel, ActiveCampaign, Zapier, webhooks, APIs, WordPress, cloud services, and a long list of tools that businesses depend on. The common thread is simple: understand the process, figure out what is breaking or wasting time, and build a better system.

AI has become another layer of that work. I use it when it can take meaningful work off someone's plate: researching information, interpreting messy inputs, monitoring data, making decisions within defined rules, calling APIs, updating systems, and moving a multi-step process toward completion.

I'm also increasingly building software with modern development tools and AI coding assistants. I'm not going to pretend that makes me a traditional senior software engineer. What I bring is a different combination: deep operational context, years of automation experience, systems thinking, and the ability to use today's tools to turn a real business problem into something that runs.

HOW I THINK ABOUT AI

Don't add a chatbot. Remove the work.

The interesting question is usually not “Where can we put AI?” It's “What work can the system take over?”

01

Start with the process

Map what people actually do, including the exceptions, handoffs, and ugly parts that rarely make it into a diagram.

02

Give AI a job

Use models where interpretation, research, classification, generation, or flexible reasoning is useful. Keep deterministic rules deterministic.

03

Connect the systems

An agent becomes useful when it can retrieve information, call APIs, update records, trigger workflows, and pass work to the next step.

04

Make it observable

Automation needs monitoring, validation, error handling, and a clear path for humans to intervene when something falls outside the rules.
